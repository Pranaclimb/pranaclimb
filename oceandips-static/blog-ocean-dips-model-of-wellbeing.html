Science & Wellbeing

An Ocean Dips Model of Wellbeing

By Annie Anderson · 10 min read

Humans did not evolve sitting indoors under artificial light.

We evolved outdoors — walking across varied landscapes, climbing rocks and trees, breathing fresh air, exposed to changing temperatures, immersed in water, and living in close relationship with the natural world. Modern lifestyles often reduce these experiences, contributing to sedentary behaviour, chronic stress, social isolation, and reduced exposure to natural environmental stimuli.

Ocean Dips was born from my own experience of restoring these ancestral inputs — through conscious breathing, coastal movement, cold-water immersion, climbing, and a deepening connection with the sea.

What I have discovered — through years of practice, guiding, and my own body — is that these elements are not separate. Each one supports and enhances the others, creating a whole-body experience that engages physiology, psychology, and our innate connection with the natural world. The combined effect is greater than the sum of its parts.

The Breath

Breathing is unique because it bridges conscious and unconscious physiology. It directly influences respiration, autonomic nervous system activity, heart rate variability, emotional regulation, attention, perception of effort, and recovery.

During Ocean Dips, breathing becomes the primary tool for responding to environmental stress. Whether entering cold water, walking the coastline, or climbing granite cliffs, the breath provides an anchor that helps regulate the nervous system and restore physiological control.

Cold Water

Cold-water immersion introduces a short-lived, controlled physiological stressor. Initial immersion activates the sympathetic nervous system, increasing breathing frequency, heart rate, and alertness. Through repeated exposure and controlled breathing, I have watched people — and experienced myself — developing cold habituation: smaller physiological responses, greater emotional control, and a quiet confidence that carries far beyond the water.

Rather than fighting the cold, participants learn to remain calm within it.

Movement

Walking, scrambling, swimming, and climbing provide natural, functional movement that supports cardiovascular health, muscular strength, coordination, balance, and mobility. Unlike repetitive gym-based exercise, movement along the coastline constantly changes, requiring continuous adaptation to rocks, tides, gradients, and terrain.

Movement prepares the body for immersion, supports recovery afterwards, and reconnects us with the way humans have moved through natural landscapes for thousands of years.

Climbing

Climbing adds a unique dimension by combining physical movement with decision-making, balance, problem-solving, and emotional regulation. Every route presents a new challenge, engaging the body and mind simultaneously.

Research suggests climbing is associated with improvements in self-confidence, attention, decision-making, resilience, emotional regulation, and psychological wellbeing. The combination of movement, controlled breathing, exploration, perceived risk, reward, and focused attention creates an immersive experience that many climbers describe as deeply meaningful and restorative.

Climbing on Cornwall's granite sea cliffs adds another layer to this experience, connecting participants with one of Britain's most iconic natural climbing environments while fostering confidence, trust, and a sense of achievement.

Nature

The coastline provides far more than scenery.

Natural environments reduce psychological stress, restore attention, improve mood, and encourage a deeper connection with the environment. The sound of breaking waves, sea air, expansive horizons, and changing light engage multiple sensory systems simultaneously, helping restore mental resources depleted by modern life.

Many participants report feeling calmer before they even enter the water.

The Interaction

The power of Ocean Dips lies not in any single activity, but in the interaction between them.

Breathing regulates the body's response to cold.

Cold heightens awareness of the breath.

Movement prepares the body for immersion and supports recovery.

Climbing develops confidence, focus, and emotional regulation.

Nature provides the restorative environment in which all of these experiences unfold.

Together, these elements create a dynamic physiological and psychological experience that I know — from the inside — is greater than the sum of its individual parts.

What I Have Discovered

My experience — and the experience of everyone I have guided into this water, up these cliffs, and along this coastline — is that integrating conscious breathing, cold-water immersion, natural movement, climbing, and coastal nature immersion produces something that exceeds the benefits of each element in isolation. Through repeated exposure, I have seen people develop improved autonomic regulation, cardiovascular health, emotional resilience, self-confidence, recovery capacity, and a deeper connection with both nature and themselves.
